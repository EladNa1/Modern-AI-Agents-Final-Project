How it works

A Reflection Agent — grade, then self-critique

Six stages. Each grade is grounded in the retrieved official solution, sampled for self-consistency, critiqued by a reflector, and escalated to a human when the agent can't grade fairly. Every call is logged in the steps[] trace on the Grade tab.

1Parser

Vision OCR reads each page, transcribes the student's work faithfully — including which option is circled — and reads the exam's identity off the cover.

2Router

Two autonomous decisions: a scope guard that politely refuses anything that isn't a Calculus 1 grading task (zero tokens spent), and scoping grading to the identified exam — the exam identity comes from the scan, not from the answer.

3Retriever

Pulls the matching official solution (exact metadata match, with a deterministic content-overlap fallback) plus supporting lecture-note chunks.

4Grader

Scores the student's actual method with partial credit. Self-consistency: N samples, median score, escalate when the samples disagree.

5Reflector

Critiques the proposed grade against the official solution and the transcript — approve, escalate, or send the critique back to the Grader for a regrade (the classic Reflection loop, max N passes). Skipped on all-or-nothing items.

6GradeBook

Accumulates per-question entries and decides completion arithmetically from the exam manifest — a total is never reported on missing questions.

Design rules

Why the grades hold up

Grounded

Every judgement ties to the official solution and course notes — no invented rubric, no invented answers.

Self-checked

Median of N independent grades; the reflector critiques against evidence, and a revision can never clear an escalation.

Fail-safe

Illegible work, missing solutions, or disagreeing samples escalate to a TA — flagged, never silently guessed.

Budget-aware

Each booklet is parsed once and cached; cost is tracked per stage with a hard per-run ceiling.

Known limits, stated plainly: the evaluation corpus is two booklets of one paper — these numbers are a regression tripwire, not a population estimate (python scripts/regression_check.py re-checks the bundled captures against the human ground truth). The once-contested Q2b key was settled by an offline SymPy check (0 real solutions); a scan re-audit then confirmed the human's 7/10 there is a legitimate sign-slip deduction, so the question is measured like any other. The grader's known weak spot is calibration on proof questions; the roadmap is few-shot calibration data ingested from more graded booklets, not more prompt rules.

The problem

Grading Calculus by hand doesn't scale

⏳Slow feedback

Students wait days to learn where they went wrong — long after they've moved on.

⚖️Inconsistent credit

Partial credit drifts between graders and across a long stack: same mistake, different score.

🔇Missing feedback

Often just a number lands, with no explanation of what went wrong or how to improve.
